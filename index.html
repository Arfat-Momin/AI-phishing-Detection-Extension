What is Phishing?

The Deceptive Art of Digital Theft

Phishing is a cybercrime where attackers, disguised as a trustworthy entity in an electronic communication, attempt to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information. This can include usernames, passwords, credit card details, or other confidential data. They use deceptive emails, websites, and text messages to lure unsuspecting victims, often creating a sense of urgency or fear to manipulate them into acting quickly.

Anatomy of an Attack

Know Your Enemy: Common Phishing Types

🎣

Deceptive Phishing

The most common type, where attackers impersonate a legitimate company to steal login credentials. These emails are sent out to a wide audience.

🎯

Spear Phishing

A targeted attack that uses personal information about the victim to appear more credible. This requires more research by the attacker.

🐋

Whaling

A form of spear phishing aimed at high-profile targets like CEOs or executives, often with the goal of financial fraud or data theft.

📱

Smishing

Phishing conducted via SMS text messages. Attackers send links to fake websites or prompt users to call fraudulent numbers.

📞

Vishing

Voice phishing, where attackers use phone calls and voice messages to create a sense of urgency and trick victims into giving up info.

🔗

Pharming

A technical attack that redirects a legitimate website's traffic to a fake site by compromising DNS servers or a user's computer.

Simple Steps for Powerful Protection

🛡️

Be Skeptical of Senders

Always check the sender's email address. Is it from a legitimate domain?

🔍

Hover, Don't Click

Before clicking any links, hover your mouse over them to see the actual destination URL.

🔐

Use Two-Factor Authentication

2FA adds a critical layer of security, even if your password is stolen.

✍️

Watch for Poor Grammar

Legitimate companies rarely send emails with glaring spelling or grammatical errors.
